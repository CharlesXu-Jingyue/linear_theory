How each chapter works

Every core topic is worked in the same order, so the reasoning is visible and not only the result. The stages appear in the text as small run-in labels; definitions and theorems sit in tinted boxes, in textbook style, and are numbered by chapter.

  1. ProblemThe question, in one sentence.
  2. Setting and assumptionsThe spaces, the field, what is given and what is not.
  3. GoalWhat an answer must deliver.
  4. ProbeThe simplest, extreme and degenerate cases, and counterexamples to tempting claims.
  5. PatternThe invariants, symmetries and equivalences that the probes expose.
  6. Conjecture, then theoremThe pattern stated precisely, with hypotheses.
  7. DeductionA short proof, or a pointer to an open source when the proof is long.
  8. VisualAn interactive figure or toy example with "try this" prompts.

Curriculum

Part I is review and moves fast, ending with the singular value decomposition (SVD). Parts II to IV carry the weight for control and dynamics, through the linear quadratic regulator (LQR), the Kalman filter and linear quadratic Gaussian (LQG) control to robustness. Part V turns the toolkit on neural circuits and on linear methods for nonlinear problems. Each card gives the chapter's core question.

Reading the figures

  • Round handles can be dragged. Presets jump to the special cases named in the text: the probes and counterexamples are one click away.
  • Readouts show the quantities a theorem relates, often both sides of an inequality, so you can watch it hold while you drag.
  • Each figure ends with Try this (things to do, with the expected observation) and What to notice (the theorem behind it).
  • Colours keep their roles across the guide: first object, ranges and reachable sets; second object; solutions, projections, stable outcomes; the moving state or test vector; null spaces, errors, instability; targets and right-hand sides.
  • Everything is computed live from the mathematics in a small linear algebra library (eigenvalues, singular values, matrix exponentials, Lyapunov and Riccati solvers); nothing is a redrawn picture.
